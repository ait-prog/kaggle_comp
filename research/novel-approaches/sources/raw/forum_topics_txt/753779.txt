**TL;DR**

- I audited every passage in the public pool that most of us train on: 254k passages from 59 community datasets.
  **About 1 in 4 passages a model learns from is junk or the wrong genre.**
- I trained the same small LoRA model on 45,000 passages each time and changed only *which* passages it saw.
  **Cleaned data scored +0.026 to +0.027 balanced accuracy on real train passages. Both seeds agree.**
- The dirtier a label's data was, the more cleaning helped (Spearman 0.64). kimi-k3 gained **+16 points of recall**
  and claude-opus-4-8 **+13**.
- **[Open Pool v9](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/crtproduction/name-that-llm-open-pool)** is that clean set:
  **192,926 passages, 18 labels, ready to train.** No filtering needed. The 70,558 removed rows are listed by `id`
  and reason in `removed_rows.csv`.

**Please support this work and share your thinking. This research cost me a lot of GPU time and effort, all to build the best training data I can for the community. If I got anything wrong, please point it out and tell me your view.**

## 1. What is wrong with the raw pool

I compared the pool with the competition's `train.csv`. This is the only data known to come from the host's own
generation process. Each label is weighted equally.
![](https://www.googleapis.com/download/storage/v1/b/kaggle-forum-message-attachments/o/inbox%2F20960131%2Fec27f8082ece67da819eba69de8fd2d9%2Ffig3_style_gap.png?generation=1793267011498326&alt=media)

| In the raw pool (v7) | Passages |
|---|---|
| Raw API responses (HTTP headers, JSON, `x-generation-id`) stored as text | 13,114 |
| Refusals ("I can't…", "As an AI…") | 6,939 |
| Cut off mid-sentence | 15,963 |
| Chat preambles ("Sure, here's…"), CJK text, length outside 36–275 words, same text with two labels | 3,545 |
| Off-genre sources: chat logs, benchmark answers, reasoning traces | 46,672 |

Two of these deserve a closer look:

- **API dumps fill the model's context window with headers.** 85% of the text you can pull out of these dumps is
  already in the pool as clean rows. You lose nothing by dropping them.
- **Off-genre text looks clean, but it teaches the wrong voice.** A label's chat replies are not the same as that
  label's single-paragraph essays. In a balanced 45k sample of the raw pool, **27.7%** of passages came from these
  sources. For some Claude labels it was more than half.

Real train passages never contain these. In train, 99.9% of passages end with punctuation; in the raw pool, 93% do.
After cleaning the figure is 99.4%.

## 2. The test: same size, different quality

I wanted to measure quality only, so every run had the same setup:

- **Model:** Qwen3-1.7B-Base with a LoRA classifier, trained for 1 epoch on the pool only.
- **Data:** exactly **2,500 passages per label (45,000 total)** in every run.
- **Score:** balanced accuracy on **all 2,699 train passages**. The model never trains on them, so every prompt is unseen.
- **Repeats:** 2 seeds per variant. Runs are deterministic: the same variant and seed reproduce to the 4th decimal.

![](https://www.googleapis.com/download/storage/v1/b/kaggle-forum-message-attachments/o/inbox%2F20960131%2F27c1fddcd2813253f4aaa4ed512683ff%2Ffig1_clean_vs_raw.png?generation=1793267031072548&alt=media)

| Training data (45k passages each) | Balanced acc. | vs raw |
|---|---|---|
| Raw pool v1-5 | 0.466 | — |
| Raw pool v1-7 | 0.464 | −0.001 |
| v1-5 minus hard junk (dumps, refusals, preambles) | 0.474 | +0.009 |
| v1-5 minus junk and off-genre sources | **0.493** | **+0.027** |
| v1-7 minus junk and off-genre sources | **0.492** | **+0.026** |

**What this shows:**

1. **Hard junk hurts.** Removing it adds about +0.009.
2. **Off-genre text hurts more than obvious junk.** Removing it adds another +0.019, even though those passages
   look clean.
3. **Newer pool batches are not the problem; their junk is.** The raw v1-7 pool scores the same as raw v1-5, and the
   clean v1-7 pool scores the same as clean v1-5. If you saw a lower LB after adding newer rows, check what came with them.

## 3. It is the dirty labels that improve

![](https://www.googleapis.com/download/storage/v1/b/kaggle-forum-message-attachments/o/inbox%2F20960131%2Fd5f31718e25886286f52e5fd42f019db%2Ffig2_label_gain.png?generation=1793267073934798&alt=media)

Each dot is one label. The x-axis shows how much of that label's raw sample was junk or off-genre. The y-axis shows
how much its recall changed after cleaning. Spearman correlation is **0.64**:

- **kimi-k3:** 46% contaminated, +16 recall
- **claude-opus-4-8:** 51% contaminated, +13 recall
- **Labels that were already clean** (most Gemini labels): little change

This is what you would expect if the junk itself was the problem.

## 4. Things we tried that did *not* help

These results are useful too, because they show what not to spend time on.

![](https://www.googleapis.com/download/storage/v1/b/kaggle-forum-message-attachments/o/inbox%2F20960131%2F07f4c7bcc437eee285919451d5f73e84%2Ffig4_round2.png?generation=1793267086475170&alt=media)

- **Keeping only the most "train-like" passages.** I built a style-only scorer that tells train passages from pool
  passages using punctuation, pronouns, sentence rhythm and function words, never topic words. It reaches AUC 0.77–0.79
  on prompts it never saw. I then dropped the 30% of each label's passages that looked least like train.
  The result was **no gain** (−0.004 balanced accuracy over 2 seeds, and −0.016 recall on the 8 hardest labels): the variety you lose costs more than the cleaner style earns.
  v9 includes the score as a column, so you can try other cut-offs yourself.
- **Dropping every passage generated through the Kaggle Benchmarks proxy.** The host has ruled that the proxy may not be
  used to build training sets ([752084]). Removing about 70k such rows changed the score by
  −0.001 on average over 2 seeds, which is within noise. v9 flags them in `flag_kb_proxy` so you can stay on the safe side at almost no cost.
- **Cheap proxies for data quality are misleading.** A TF-IDF + linear model trained on the same subsets said cleaning
  *hurts*. It rewards volume and does not care about genre. Only test data decisions with the kind of model you
  actually train.

## 5. What is still open: the prompt gap

One style gap does not move with cleaning. **31% of train passages use "we / our / us", against 15% in the pool,
before and after cleaning.** The cause is the host's prompts, not junk. Their prompts lead to a collective, explanatory
voice more often than the prompt banks most of us used. If you generate new data, prompts closer to that voice are
probably the next big win. Please share what you find.

## 6. How to use v9

```python
import pandas as pd
pool = pd.read_parquet("/kaggle/input/name-that-llm-open-pool/ntl_open_pool.parquet")
print(len(pool), pool.model.value_counts())       # 192,926 clean passages, 18 labels: train on all of it
```

- **Every row is clean.** v9 removes API dumps, refusals, preambles, off-genre sources, cut-off passages, rows with a
  licence or provenance problem, answers to train prompts, and typography a label never uses in train.
- **Row `id`s are unchanged** from earlier versions, so your existing splits keep working. Removed `id`s and the
  reason for each are in `removed_rows.csv`, and the totals per reason are in `removal_summary.csv`.
- **Licences are clean too.** Every row comes from a CC BY 4.0, CC BY-SA 4.0 or CC0 dataset with verifiable provenance.
  About 38.8k rows that came from a licence-excluded source through CC0 re-uploads are gone.
- **`flag_kb_proxy`** marks rows generated through the Kaggle Benchmarks proxy. They are kept because public community
  datasets are allowed; drop them in one line if you want to be strict (section 4).
- **`style_score`** is the train-likeness score from section 4.
- The numbers in this post ship with the dataset: `dataq_results.csv`, `style_gap.csv`, `removal_summary.csv` and the figures.

## Caveats

- These tests use a 1.7B model at a fixed 45k passages. Bigger models are more robust to noise, so the gain may be
  smaller at 14B scale. I am now training a full-size model on v9 and will report its LB score in this thread.
- The clean set has about 20% fewer passages than the raw pool. At equal size clean wins clearly; at full size I am
  still measuring.
- Balanced accuracy on train passages is not the LB. It is the closest held-out data we have, and every prompt in it is
  unseen during training.
