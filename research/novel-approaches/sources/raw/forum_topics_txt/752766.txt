# [NOW UPDATED]## Open Pool v14: real `kimi-k3` was written with reasoning on, `glm-5.3` is fine, and 284 mislabelled rows are gone
url: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/name-that-llm/discussion/752766
author: Carrot Production date: 2026-10-26 votes: {'totalVotes': 85, 'canUpvote': True, 'totalUpvotes': 85}
**[Open Pool v14](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/crtproduction/name-that-llm-open-pool)** has **223,840 passages** from 61 source datasets. This version adds 11,501 passages. More importantly, it records **how every passage was generated**, so we can finally ask which generations look like the real ones.

### How I measured it

I trained a plain TF-IDF + logistic regression classifier **only on the 2,699 real train passages**. On train prompts it never saw (5 folds grouped by prompt), it gets 33% balanced accuracy. That is weak, but it is a fixed yardstick.

I then scored every pool passage with it. If pool passages of a label are recognised much less often than real passages of that label, they differ from the real ones in a way the classifier can see. Read the results for large groups only, never to filter single passages.

### Finding 1: real `kimi-k3` was most likely written with reasoning on

Real `kimi-k3` passages are recognised 31% of the time. Pool `kimi-k3` passages, split by the new `gen_reasoning` column:

| Reasoning setting | Passages | Recognised as `kimi-k3` |
|---|---|---|
| high | 2,673 | **25%** |
| default | 2,871 | 11% |
| unknown | 2,054 | 11% |
| off | 1,702 | 8% |

This is not a source effect. Inside one source, with the same prompts and filters (willrice), reasoning high gives 21% and reasoning off gives 8%.

So only about 2,700 of the 9,700 pool `kimi-k3` passages look like the real model. If you use pool Kimi, try this subset first:

```python
kimi_real_like = pool[(pool.model == "kimi-k3") & (pool.gen_reasoning == "high")]
```

`glm-5.3` points the same way on fewer passages: reasoning low 13% against default 17%, with real at 19%. For GPT, Gemini and Claude the settings differ less and in no consistent direction. samarth1006's label audit reached a similar conclusion for Kimi from a different angle.

### Finding 2: `glm-5.3` does not need reprocessing

This answers the question asked earlier in this thread (and thread 755314). **Real** `glm-5.3` already reads like Claude: the train-only classifier puts 40% of its probability on the Claude labels. Pool `glm-5.3` does the same (42%), and its sources agree with each other. The Claude-like style belongs to the model; it is not a labelling error in the pool.

### Finding 3: where the pool is furthest from real passages

Pool recognition rate as a share of the real rate (100% = as recognisable as real):

- `kimi-k3` 46%, `qwen3.8-max` 51%, `claude-opus-4-8` 54%: the widest gaps.
- `claude-opus-4-8`: most of the gap comes from two sources, kilupy's Claude family set (3,209 passages, 24%) and adurey (441 passages, 17%).
- The GPT labels range from 79% to 102%.

Every source and label with 30 or more passages is in `fidelity_by_source.csv`.

### Correction: 284 mislabelled passages removed

In one source, the author's own model column shows that 284 passages were written by models outside the 18 labels:

| Written by | Labelled as | Passages |
|---|---|---|
| gemini-3.5-flash-lite | `gemini-3.6-flash` | 121 |
| gpt-6-luna | `gpt-5.6-luna` | 101 |
| claude-opus-4-7 | `claude-opus-4-8` | 43 |
| claude-haiku-5-5 | `claude-fable-5` | 19 |

They are out of the main file and listed in `removed_rows.csv`. I checked every model-id column in every source file and found no other case. If you merged public datasets yourself, check yours.

### What is new in the files

- **7 new columns:**
  - `gen_reasoning` (known for 54% of rows), with `gen_reasoning_evidence` and `gen_reasoning_raw`;
  - `gen_route`, `gen_served_model`, `gen_date`;
  - `flag_kb_proxy_after_ruling`: 17,876 proxy rows generated after the hosts' 2026-10-28 answer on the proxy quota. This is a lower bound, because sources that record no date are not flagged. The hosts said public datasets made this way are fine to train on; the flag is there if you want to be strict.
- **`source_protocol.csv`:** for each of the 61 sources, the protocol it declares (system prompt, instruction wording, reasoning, sampling, route, text edits after generation, served-model check), with the document it was read from.
- **`fidelity_by_label.csv`, `fidelity_by_source.csv`, `fidelity_by_reasoning.csv`:** the numbers behind this post.

### Caveats

- **None of this is tested by training yet.** It shows which passages look real, not that dropping the others raises your score. Earlier I found that dropping rows by cross-source agreement *hurt*, so test before you filter.
- **The classifier is weak** and partly sees topic, and pool prompts are not the host's prompts. Compare groups, not single passages.

**If you test the Kimi subset or the fidelity tables on the LB, please share the result here, especially if it disagrees.**

- [GoPain | votes {'canUpvote': True} | 2026-10-31] Thanks for the controlled comparison and for calling +0.002 noise. One thing that may help with reading such pairs:
the noise of a *difference* between two submissions is not the noise of one score. Both are scored on the same public
rows, so only the rows where exactly one of the two is right move the gap. If f is that share, the sd of the public
gap is about sqrt(f / n_public), which for ~1,200 public rows is:

| f (rows where exactly one is right) | 1% | 2% | 5% | 10% | 20% |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| sd of the public gap | 0.003 | 0.004 | 0.006 | 0.009 | 0.013 |

You can measure f on your OOF before submitting: compare the two models' correctness row by row.

- Two separately trained models (different pools, or even the same code with another seed) can easily disagree in
  correctness on 10-20% of rows. That gives an sd of 0.009-0.013, the size of the ~0.009 that alexchilton measured,
  and +0.002 says nothing either way, as you wrote.
- Two blends that differ in one small weight are a different story. My 0.621 and 0.623 blends disagree in
  correctness on only 1% of OOF rows, so their gap has an sd of about 0.003. There, 0.002 is still under one sd, but
  a 0.01 gap would be over three sd, i.e. real.

I checked the formula by simulation (balanced accuracy, 18 classes, random 30% public split; it matched within ±6%
everywhere) and put it in a CPU notebook, with a helper that estimates f from your own OOF predictions:
https://www.kaggle.com/code/locdunhat/is-a-0-002-lb-gain-real

Caveat: it assumes independent rows. Real errors cluster by prompt and topic, so treat these numbers as a floor.
