# From 0.684 toward top 1%: what validation evidence predicts real gains?
url: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/name-that-llm/discussion/758301
author: Pengyi Peng1 date: 2026-11-12 votes: {'canUpvote': True}
Hi everyone,

I am trying to improve a reproducible baseline toward the top 1% while avoiding
leaderboard overfitting. I would appreciate feedback on validation and model
complementarity rather than private weights or test-label heuristics.

**Current evidence, as of 12 November:** my best public score is 0.684, an attributed
reproduction of [Areeb Ali's v11](https://www.kaggle.com/code/areebbb/0-684-i-m-better?scriptVersionId=368307915),
not my original ensemble. My snapshot is 308/5575 teams; strict top 1% is rank 55,
whose displayed score is 0.705. Rank 56 is also 0.705, so a tied score is not a
guarantee. Public standings are provisional,not final medals.

**What did not establish a gain:** three small row-local residuals moved a
separate CPU component's prompt-grouped macro recall from 0.385185 to at best
0.391204. Corrected paired intervals crossed zero and sparse-prompt diagnostics
regressed. These are reused development results,not fresh holdout evidence,
and are not comparable with the full ensemble's 0.684 public score. One
exploratory variant is still being scored;its result is unknown.

I published a small [CPU Validation Lab](https://www.kaggle.com/code/pengyipeng1/name-that-llm-top1-validation-lab):
original standard-library helpers for 18-class macro recall, prompt-overlap
rejection and deterministic paired prompt-cluster intervals. Its executed
examples are synthetic only; it is not a top 1% inference model or new submission.

My proposed next experiment is ONE complementary representation/family
specialist, with matched prompt-disjoint OOF for the complete incumbent,
calibration fitted inside folds, external-source/duplicate audits and per-class
plus one/two/many-row prompt checks. Full-ensemble OOF is still missing,not a
completed validation claim. I found the earlier [grouped-CV and transfer discussion](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/name-that-llm/discussion/757855)
helpful, particularly its warning that group-based local gains may not transfer.

Questions:

1. Which prompt-held-out and external-source-held-out protocol has best
predicted transfer for you? How do you audit same-prompt regeneration and
duplicates in external data without querying/tuning on the public leaderboard?
2. With balanced accuracy and changing model priors, which fold-safe calibration,
per-class recall or family-confusion diagnostics identify genuinely complementary
embedding/LoRA models? Public,license-compatible ablations would be helpful.
3. How do you verify that the tested post-processing actually executes in the
full hidden rerun, including tail batches, rather than only the tiny preview?

I will preserve negative findings and credit shared methods. Thanks for any
specific methodological feedback or links to reproducible public work.

Pengyi Peng
